Powder Ops · Document Control

Document Set Changeover

Seventeen steps, in order, to bring the SOP set to final in Drive and get it into ReadyDoc. Work through it over the next few days and tick as you go — the ticks save in this browser.

For Daniela Servin Prepared 2 September 2026 Covers 66 SOPs · 50 forms · 28 WIs · 24 JDs

Do

  • Upload over the top of what's already in ReadyDoc. It keeps each document's revision history and shows you what changed.
  • Upload in waves as documents get signed. Don't hold the first one back waiting for the last.
  • Attach the signed PDF to each document as you go — that's the evidence behind the revision.
  • Skip with a reason where a proposed change is wrong. The reason is recorded and the item stays on the list.

Do not

  • Don't renumber SOPs to close the gaps. The form numbers hang off them and they're printed on filed records. Step 4 explains the gaps instead.
  • Don't delete anything from ReadyDoc. Withdraw it with a reason — a deleted document looks like one that never existed.
  • Don't empty the register and re-upload. Every document would look like it was created this week.
  • Don't upload from FSQA - Upload. It's a revision behind on 33 SOPs.
Part A

Fix the master set on Drive

~1 hour

Do this before anything is uploaded. Four documents are missing from the master folders and one filename will not parse.

01 Copy four missing SOPs back into their master folders

These four folders in 1. SOP_S / Series 400 are empty, but the documents exist in FSQA - Upload / SOP_s. Copy each one across:

  • SOP 411 V2 — Product/Documentation Disposal
  • SOP 423 V2 — Visitor Policy
  • SOP 425 V2 — Material Handling and Staging
  • SOP 439 V1 — Food Defense Plan

Why. The master folder is meant to be the one place the current document lives. Right now four numbers are issued with nothing behind them, and the only copy is in a folder we're about to retire.

02 Fix the SOP 205 filename

Rename SOP V2 205 SHIPPING.docx to SOP 205 V2 SHIPPING.docx. The number and the revision are the wrong way round.

Why. Every other file in the set reads number, revision, title. ReadyDoc reads the document number off the filename, so this one won't match its registry row and will come in as a new document instead of a revision.

03 Check each master folder holds one current file

Several folders hold two or three copies of the same document — a .docx and a .pdf, or an old revision beside the new one. That's fine, but make sure it's obvious which one is current. Where an old revision is kept, move it to an Archive subfolder.

Watch for: SOP 100 has three files, SOP 111 has V3 and V5 side by side, SOP 426 has V2 and V3, SOP 431 has V2 and V3.

05 Write the rule into SOP 400

Add one sentence to SOP 400 Publishing, Updating and Control of SOPs: a new procedure takes the next number after the highest in its series, and a number recorded as never issued is never reused.

Why. Without it, someone tidy-minded fills SOP 407 next year and contradicts the line you just wrote in the log.

Part D

Finalise, sign, upload — in waves

the long one · repeat per wave

Steps 10–15 are a loop. Do a batch of documents, upload them, then start the next batch. Tick these once the last wave is in.

10 Take a batch of documents to final in Drive

Work in whatever order suits you. Ten or fifteen at a time is a good wave — enough to be worth an upload, small enough to finish.

Don't wait for all 66. Until a document is uploaded, ReadyDoc is quoting its old revision. Six weeks of waiting is six weeks of that.

11 Get each one signed and authorised

Keep the signed PDF — you'll attach it in step 14, and it's the evidence that the revision was approved rather than just typed.

12 Upload the wave into ReadyDoc

Go to Document Control → Review Center. Use Update from file and drop the whole wave in at once — it takes multiple files.

ReadyDoc reads each file, works out which registry document it is, and builds a list of proposed changes. Nothing is applied yet.

The list survives. It's saved, not a pop-up — you can close the tab, come back tomorrow, and it's still there with your progress on it. Half-finished is a fine place to stop.

13 Work the list from the top down

It's ordered so the ones that matter are first:

  • Revision moved — the registry is quoting a superseded revision. These are the real ones.
  • Couldn't match the file — it doesn't correspond to a registry document. Usually a filename problem.
  • Dates only / title only / body only — smaller corrections.
  • No change — the registry already agrees. Nothing to do.

Each change is already ticked. Untick anything you don't want applied, then apply.

Expect a lot of “revision moved” on the first wave. Comparing the current master set against what was uploaded before, 33 SOPs are one revision ahead of the older copy — 111 is V5 against V4, 401 is V4 against V3, 500 is V4 against V3, and thirty more. That's the point of doing this.

14 Attach the signed PDF to each document

Open the document and use the attachments block. It's on both the read view and the edit view, so whichever way you got in, it's there.

15 Skip anything that's wrong — with a reason

If a proposed change is wrong, skip it rather than unticking and moving on. A skip asks for a reason and records who skipped it, so the item is accounted for instead of just absent.

Don't delete a document to get rid of it. If something in ReadyDoc genuinely no longer applies, withdraw it — it asks for a reason, keeps the history, and prints “NO LONGER IN USE” across the top so a printout can't pass as current. A deleted document looks exactly like one that never existed, which is the first thing an auditor asks about.
Part E

Close out

~10 minutes

16 Retire the FSQA - Upload folder

Once the master set is in ReadyDoc, rename that folder to ARCHIVE — pre-Sept 2026 upload snapshot and move it out of the working area.

Why. It's a second copy of the truth and it's already a revision behind on 33 documents. As long as it's sitting there looking like the upload folder, somebody will upload from it.

17 Note anything ReadyDoc has that Drive doesn't

As you work the list, you'll see registry documents that no file ever matched — the old-facility carryovers. Write them down rather than acting on them. Don't withdraw anything on a hunch; bring the list back and we'll go through it together.

One known candidate: FORM 402 V2 Raw Material Product Inspection, which is already in the ARCHIVE folder on Drive but may still be live in ReadyDoc.
