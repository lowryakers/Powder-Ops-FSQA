# Master SOP Log — draft entries for the thirteen unissued numbers

**Draft for Document Control (Daniela Servin) · 2 September 2026**

The SOP series has thirteen numbers that were never issued at this facility. They are the residue of
copying a document set from another plant and leaving out what did not apply — the numbers were never
closed up, so the sequence has holes in it.

**The recommendation is to explain the holes, not to close them.** SQF Edition 9 asks that documents be
identified, current, approved and retrievable; it does not ask that they be sequential. Renumbering
would move every form that hangs off an SOP (FORM 431-01 and 431-02 belong to SOP 431), and those
numbers are printed on filed records, cited in 159 change requests and matched by the software.
A number here is retired, never reissued.

An unexplained gap invites the question. A gap with a line against it in the Master Log **is** the
answer, and it took twenty minutes instead of a migration.

---

## The evidence

Every one of these thirteen numbers appears **nowhere** in the plant's document set:

- 0 occurrences across **1,016 files** in the SOP / FORM / WI / JD folders
- 0 occurrences across **159 Document Change Requests** (0001–0159, no gaps in that register)

So the statement "not issued at this facility" is a finding, not an assumption. Nothing was issued
under these numbers and then lost.

## Where you can make an entry stronger

Where the original topic is remembered, naming it and naming what covers that ground here turns a
statement into a decision. For example:

> **SOP 407** — Not issued. Content of the inherited procedure is covered by SOP 406 Documentation and
> Record Keeping; no separate document was adopted.

That is a better entry, because it answers the follow-up question as well as the first one. **Only
write it where it is actually true** — the uniform line above is correct and defensible on its own, and
an invented rationale is worse than none.

---

## What NOT to do

- **Do not renumber the existing SOPs to close the gaps.** The cost lands on the forms, the filed
  records and the change-request register, and buys nothing an auditor asks for.
- **Do not reuse a gap number for a new procedure.** These numbers now have a line in the log saying
  they were never issued; issuing one later contradicts the log. New procedures take the next number
  after the highest in their series.
- **Do not delete the gap lines once written.** They are the explanation; without them the holes are
  back.
