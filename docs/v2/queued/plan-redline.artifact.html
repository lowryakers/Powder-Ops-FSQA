FSP-02The Rework row describes a control the plant does not havemust Hazard Analysis → Rework · already DCR item 2
Metal detection at the metal detection process step during tub filling. X ray is inspected finished good.

V3's own revision note reads "Removed any Magnetic Detection information", and the plant runs an X-ray, not a metal detector. Residue of the V1 change that added magnets. An auditor will ask to be shown the metal-detection step at tub filling, and there is not one.

FSP-03Screens: the chart and the Process Description state different controlsmust PC #3 vs Process Description → Screens · already DCR item 1

Chart: "Freq: at the beginning of every machine start up."
Process Description: "The mesh size (50 mesh or 70 mesh) is recorded as well as the condition of the screen at the beginning and at the end of each batch."

The second is stricter and names two facts the chart does not — mesh size used and screen condition. A screen intact at start-up and torn at the end is precisely what this control exists to catch, and only the second reading catches it.

Recommendation: adopt the Process Description's wording into the chart.
FSP-04PC #1's critical limit is stated in a unit only its verification step producesmust PC #1 · already DCR item 3

Critical limit "No more than 35 RLU"; monitoring is "Application of cleaning. Visual inspection prior to set up"; verification is "ATP swabs and visual inspection."

RLU is what an ATP swab reads — nothing else in the process produces one. So the plan sets a numeric limit, monitors by eye, and puts the instrument that reads the number on the verification leg. Whichever leg carries the number is the leg software can enforce.

Recommendation: move the ATP swab to monitoring, leave visual inspection as verification. This also matches the Process Description: "Product contact surfaces are swabbed using ATP swabs prior [to] the start of a production run."
FSP-05Three signature lines are blank on a document marked Approved / Effectivemust Cover page

SQF Practitioner, Preventive Controls Qualified Individual and Plant Manager are all unsigned, while the document's status is Approved / Effective with an effective date of 2026-06-25. One of the first things an auditor checks.

FSP-06The QA/QC Manager is named differently in the two plansmust Preventive Control Food Safety Team

FSP-07The revision history uses two numbering schemesmust Revision History

Row one is 0; every row after is V1…V4. Make the first row V0, or restate it as "Original issue".

FSP-08The plan names records but never says where they are keptmust Preventive Control Chart → Record Keeping

All four controls point at the batch production record or a document attached to it. Those records are on paper today, logged in MRPEasy — not in Keychain, not in ReadyDoc. Content and standards · 11

FSP-23The plan cites no regulation anywhereshould

Neither plan contains the string "21 CFR". The hazard analysis is laid out in the standard 21 CFR 117 preventive-controls format and the plant holds PCQI certificates — name the basis in a Scope section, along with 21 CFR 111, the dietary-supplement CGMP that NSF/ANSI 455-2 is built on.

FSP-24No reanalysis trigger other than the annual reviewshould

The plan should say when it is reanalysed other than on a schedule: a change in process, equipment, supplier or product; a newly identified hazard; and after any failure of a preventive control. As written, a new piece of equipment could arrive without anything requiring the plan to be revisited.

FSP-25Foreign Supplier Verification is not addressedshould

21 CFR Part 1 Subpart L is a normative reference for NSF/ANSI 455-2. Neither plan mentions FSVP or imported ingredients. If any ingredient is imported, this is a named gap. If none is, one sentence saying so closes it.

FSP-26Sanitary Transportation is not addressedshould

21 CFR Part 1 Subpart O is also a normative reference for 455-2. The Food Defense Plan discusses transport as a security vulnerability; neither plan addresses transport as a sanitation control — carrier requirements, temperature, cleanliness of the conveyance, records.

FSP-28Banned and prohibited substances are not referencedmust3 nonconformancespromoted 27 Aug 2026 — confirmed by the GMP for Sport audit
Confirmed by audit. The GMP for Sport audit of 24–26 Aug raised three minor nonconformances, and they are exactly these three sections: §6.2.2, §6.2.3.1 and §6.2.3.2 — raised here from the Audit Guide on file, before the reports arrived.

NSF GMP for Sport §6.2.2 requires that references to the NSF 306 Annex C, NFL/NFLPA, MLB and WADA lists be "incorporated as part of their applicable operating procedures", and §6.2.3.1 requires a documented annual review of those lists. A draft Banned and Prohibited Substance Control Program already sits in the registry (SOP-DRAFT-BSC) — reference it once Document Control assigns a number.

FSP-29Record retention is not statedshould

21 CFR 111.605 sets retention at one year past shelf life or two years past distribution, whichever is longer. The plan names records and never says how long they are kept.

FSP-34"CCP" and "preventive control" are used interchangeablyshould

The chart says Preventive Control; V2's revision note says "Added X-ray as a CCP"; Policy 002 calls it "a validated HACCP-based approach". All three are defensible and should be deliberate. This is the first item of the vocabulary pass.

FSP-30 · Allergen program consider PC #2 turns on allergen control and the Process Description describes real practice — end-of-batch scooping, gown changes, a full room clean verified by swab, a dedicated storage location. None of it is a referenced document.
FSP-31 · Supply-chain control consider Several rows justify "No" on supplier qualification and letters of guarantee — the reasoning behind a supply-chain preventive control, a category 117 names. The chart has none.
FSP-32 · "the missing ingredient" consider "Rework … is allowed if the amount of the missing ingredient is known." Read plainly this is about a spill. Spilled or lost would say it.
FSP-27Electronic records are not mentionedmustnonconformance 4.4.39 promoted 27 Aug 2026 — this one names ReadyDoc

21 CFR Part 11 is a normative reference for 455-2, and ReadyDoc holds a growing share of these records with e-signatures, an append-only audit trail and revocation. The plan should say records may be kept electronically, and name the system.

Confirmed by audit, and the wording is generous. Finding 4.4.39: "While software applications ReadyDoc and MRP Easy utilize functional compliance features such as unique user logins and audit trails, formal documentation demonstrating validation of the systems was not available." The features are there; the documentation is not. Naming electronic records in the plan is the document half — the validation package is the technology half.

The FDA Intentional Adulteration rule, 21 CFR Part 121, is the basis of a food defense plan for a registered facility. Naming it — alongside the FDA registration number the plan already carries — makes the plan self-evidently responsive.

FDP-23No Food Defense Qualified Individual is namedshould

Part 121 requires the vulnerability assessment and mitigation strategies be prepared by, or overseen by, a qualified individual. Carol Rojas holds an AIB Food Defense Coordinator certificate, which is on file — the plan lists her as Plan Coordinator without naming the qualification. State it.

FDP-25The mock incident has no form and no recordshould

§9.2 requires a mock incident at least once every two years. Nothing in the Forms Master Index answers to it and no record exists. The plan's own verification of itself is the part with no evidence behind it.

FDP-29The highest-scored mitigation has no recordshould

§5.4 mitigates intentional adulteration by an employee — joint-highest risk at 15 — with "Require employees to never work alone and adequate monitoring of footage." Nothing records whether anyone worked alone, and nothing records that footage was reviewed. A mitigation strategy with no monitoring record is the gap Part 121 is most pointed about.

FDP-30The risk matrix has no scoring keyshould

SQF-05The methodology for determining hazard significance is not documentedmust2.4.3.8
"The methodology for determining hazard significance shall be documented and used consistently to assess all potential hazards."

The hazard analysis answers YES/NO per hazard with a justification and never states how significance was decided — no severity, no likelihood, no matrix, no decision tree.

The sharpest illustration is internal: the Food Defense Plan has a documented risk matrix (likelihood × impact, 1–5). The Food Safety Plan has nothing equivalent. One of the plant's two plans already does what this clause asks.
SQF-06The critical limits are not validated, and there is no annual re-validationmust2.4.3.11 · 2.5.1.1(ii)
"The food safety team shall validate all of the critical limits…" · "Critical food safety limits are reviewed annually and re-validated or justified by regulatory standards when changes occur."

35 RLU. 50 or 70 mesh. NFe 2 mm, Fe 2 mm, stainless steel 4 mm, ceramic 2 mm, glass 2 mm. Every one is a number the plan asserts and does not justify.

Validation is a distinct activity from the verification the chart already lists. It answers "why is 35 the right number?", not "was it met?" — and records of validation activities must be maintained.
SQF-07PC #1 and PC #2 corrective actions are incompletemust2.4.3.13
"…deviation procedures that identify the disposition of affected product … shall also prescribe actions to correct the process step to prevent recurrence."

PC #1 and PC #2 say only "Re-clean line." Two of three required elements are missing: what happens to product already made, and what stops it recurring. Clause 2.5.3.1 adds a third — root cause, and "re-clean" identifies nothing.

PC #3 and PC #4 already do this properly — segregate and hold, investigate disposition, identify the source, fix the equipment; 100 % inspection back to the last good check. The plant knows how to write this. Two of its four controls simply do not.
SQF-10Allergen management is a Mandatory clause with a six-part content listmust2.8.1 · promotes FSP-30

  • an assessment of workplace allergens from locker rooms, vending machines, lunchrooms and visitors — the item nobody thinks of, and it is mandatory;
  • an allergen list for country of manufacture and destination, accessible to staff;

2.8.1.5 requires documented validation and verification of allergen cleaning effectiveness — that is PC #2, and it is unvalidated. 2.8.1.10 requires reworked product containing allergens to be clearly identified and traceable; the plan gives rework its own process step and says nothing about it.
