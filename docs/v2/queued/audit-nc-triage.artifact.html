The headline, and it decides the plan. Eleven of the twelve findings are phrased as a document or a record that does not exist, or was not available. In the auditor's own words:

"…are not established" × 4
"was not available" × 3
"were not conducted" · "not prepared" · "not provided at the time of the audit" · "evidence … was not available"

Only one — 4.2.9, street shoes in GMP areas — describes something the plant does that it should not. Everything else is work whose evidence is missing, or a procedure nobody has written down. That is precisely the failure mode this platform exists to prevent, and it is the same thing the preventive-control walk found from the other direction.

So the split is not "software versus people". It is three buckets.

A  ReadyDoc is the fix. The missing thing is a record, a gate or a program. Build it and the finding closes. — 5 findings

B  ReadyDoc makes it stick. A person must do the work. ReadyDoc raises the obligation, catches the result, and makes a miss visible instead of silent. — 4 findings

C  Outside ReadyDoc. A document to write, a study to run, a behaviour to change. — 3 findings

What this is. All twelve findings from the August audit, sorted by who fixes it and how rather than by which standard they came from.

The three groups. A — the missing thing is a record or a control, and building it in ReadyDoc closes the finding. B — a person has to do the work; ReadyDoc’s job is to ask for it, catch the result, and make a miss obvious instead of silent. C — outside ReadyDoc entirely: a document to write, a study to run, or a habit to change on the floor.

The one thing to take away: eleven of the twelve findings are phrased as a document or a record that did not exist, or could not be produced when asked for. Only one is about something we do that we should not.

4.5.43No MMR per formulation and batch sizeAthe big one
Not started
"A blank MMR template is established, 413-1 Packaging Batch Production Record; however, there is not an MMR established and approved by Quality for each formula and batch size." [21 CFR 111.205(a)]

The standing headline of the gap analysis, now confirmed by an auditor. Note the precise finding: the template exists, the per-formula approved masters do not.

MMR — per formulation and batch size: components with identity and weight, theoretical yield, specifications at each control point, the procedure for each step, approved and signed by Quality before use. BPR — the executed version: actual weights, component lots, who performed and who verified each step at the time, in-process results against spec, actual versus theoretical yield, and the release decision.

Sequence matters. The MMR is the smaller build and it is the finding. The BPR is larger and depends on it. It should link to the Production Log rather than replace it — the same relationship Flavor Approval and Organoleptic already have.

4.3.1 · Supplier qualification questionnaires

A Register built · finding still open

What was built, and it is real. Before this there was no supplier record in ReadyDoc of any kind — the chemical registry holds chemicals, the certifications module holds people. There is now a Supplier Qualification register carrying the SOP's own three dispositions and seven risk criteria, transcribed rather than paraphrased.

  • 75 suppliers on the register, reconciled between Purchasing's tracker and the document archive — which disagreed about 48 of them.
  • 938 documents uploaded from the Drive archive and stored, searchable by the text inside each PDF. "Show me the questionnaire" now ends in a document.
  • Expiry dates read from the documents themselves. Ten certificates on file had already expired and nothing knew.
  • The annual review raises its own work order 30 days before it is due, so a review date is no longer a date nobody watches.
  • Nothing was imported as qualified. Every supplier arrives "not qualified"; a completed questionnaire is evidence for a disposition, not the disposition.

And it did not close the finding. The auditor named three suppliers. Checked against the archive as loaded: Mill Haven Foods has two documents on file and both are specification sheets, not a questionnaire. M4 Dynamic and Bay State Milling have no folder in the archive at all. All three gaps the auditor recorded are still open — what has changed is that they are now provable rather than unknown.

Where the register stands as a whole. 43 suppliers are actively used with no approved disposition. That splits two ways, and the split is the useful part:

22 can be dispositioned today — evidence already on file
21 have no questionnaire to decide against

The first 22 are a working session for Quality, now queued in the app with each supplier's documents in the same window as the decision. The second 21 — and the three the auditor named — are a chase Purchasing has to run with the suppliers themselves. No software can ask a supplier for a questionnaire.

What we would tell the auditor today. One of twelve findings has had substantial work, and it is not closed. The systemic half — a register, the evidence retrievable, expiries watched, the annual review generating work — is done and can be demonstrated live. The half that closes 4.3.1 is collecting questionnaires from named suppliers, and that is a purchasing activity with a date on it, not a build.

Why this section says "0 closed". A finding is closed when the corrective action is complete and the evidence is in front of somebody who asks for it. Marking 4.3.1 closed because the register exists would be exactly the kind of record this platform is being built to stop us keeping.
