That produced W2-01 to W2-19. The second pass, added once all four documents were supplied, is the wording review the first could not do: W2-20 to W2-46.

The second pass did not overturn the first. Every D-002 finding held. What the documents added is worse than the tables suggested, in one repeated way: where ReadyDoc has no record, the SOP usually has no form number either. The gap is not that the software fell behind the documents — both are thin in the same places.

Three findings would be found by an auditor in the first ten minutes, and none of them is about software.

None of the four documents is signed — every Dept. Approval and Q.A. Approval block is empty, four for four (W2-20). Every one disagrees with its own revision history about its current revision date — SOP 434 by ten months (W2-21). And one piece of genuinely good news. These SOPs cite other documents by number — SOP 404 cites SOP 607; SOP 604 cites SOP 600 and SOP 601. D-027 stopped the document reference graph because the two plans cite nothing at all. The registry’s SOPs are a better starting corpus for that graph than the plans were.

What this is. A read of the four SOPs the auditor picked up and found wanting. It asks one question of each: does this SOP require something we have no way to produce?

The three legs. Every requirement needs three things to be real — a programme (the SOP saying to do it), a form (a numbered form to record it on), and a record (the filled-in evidence). If any leg is missing, the requirement exists on paper and nowhere else. That is what the coloured boxes below show.

Severities work as in the plan red-line: MUST is wrong as a matter of fact, SHOULD undermines the document, CONSIDER is a judgement call you may decline. Every finding is numbered so you can take them one at a time.

Part 1 · Document control

These four came out of comparing the auditor’s citations against the plant’s own Document Change Request log — 159 rows, read in full. They needed no SOP text, and they are the most immediately actionable findings here.

W2-01Neither SOP 421 nor SOP 434 appears anywhere in the DCR logMust

The auditor read both and cited each by revision and effective date. The change log — every new document and every revision since May 2025 — has no row for either. Not a New Document row, not a New Revision row.

Two explanations, both of which matter. Either they were issued outside the DCR process, which is a document-control nonconformance waiting to be written; or the log is incomplete, which means it cannot answer the question it exists to answer. An auditor who asks to see the change history for SOP 434 gets nothing today.

I would fix this one first. It costs a morning, and it is the kind of gap an auditor finds in five minutes.

Verified — all 159 rows read; the only “421” and “434” in that file are digits inside a phone number and a product code

W2-04Nothing reconciles the change log against the document registryShould

The DCR log imports into the QMS register as change-request records. The document registry is the document registry, a different table, populated by upload. Nothing compares them, in either direction.

That is why W2-01 and W2-02 survived: a document can sit in the registry with no change history, or have a change history and never reach the registry, and no screen reports either. The recurring problem this whole project keeps finding — the same fact kept in two places, with nobody owning either — this time at the level of the document register itself.

Two rules in the mechanical sweep close it: in the log, absent from the registry, and its inverse. Cheap, and it runs forever after.

Part 2 · SOP 404 — Supplier and Laboratory Qualification

“The Supplier Qualification Questionnaire required per SOP 404 was not available for: Mill Haven Foods (Whey Protein), M4 Dynamic (Potassium Citrate, B12), Bay State Milling (Cinnamon).” NSF/ANSI 455-2 § 4.3.1 · 21 CFR 117.405 & 117.410
W2-05There is no supplier record in ReadyDoc of any kindMust

Not a thin one — none. the Approved Chemicals list holds chemicals. the Certifications module (which holds people’s certificates) holds people (person_name NOT NULL). supply_orders and supply_invoices are office purchasing. Nothing anywhere holds a supplier, an ingredient it supplies, a risk evaluation, a qualification decision, an approver, or a certificate with an expiry date.

So the finding is not “the questionnaire was misfiled”. There is no system it could have been filed in. Three named the supplier register of three named ingredients, and the answer to “show me their qualification” is a question about somebody’s folders.

W2-06The laboratory half has no record at all — and the code already assumes otherwiseMust

the COA requests log.lab_name is a free-text field. There is no laboratory record, no accreditation, no scope, no expiry; ISO/IEC 17025 — the standard that actually matters for a testing laboratory — is not mentioned anywhere in ReadyDoc.

Worse, ReadyDoc already leans on an accreditation it does not hold. the step that grades a lab result decides how a lab result is graded on this reasoning:

“The lab’s own verdict wins. It is the accredited party and it applied the …”

A correct principle resting on a fact nothing records. An auditor asking “how did you qualify CTLA, and against what scope?” gets a text field somebody typed. Given the title now explicitly covers laboratories (W2-02), this is squarely in scope and squarely missing.

W2-07The expiring-certificate pattern is bound to a person, and that decision is due nowShould

the Certifications module (which holds people’s certificates) already does most of what supplier certificates need — issuer, number, issued and expiry dates, the file, and an expiry that generates work. But a person’s name is always required, so an organic, kosher, GFSI or allergen certificate cannot be filed against a company without widening that table or creating a second one.

Decide before building, not during — it is the difference between supplier qualification being a new module and an extension of one that works. My reading: a new table, because a supplier carries a risk evaluation and an approval decision that a person’s certificate does not — but the certificate half should reuse the existing expiry machinery rather than grow a second copy of it.

W2-08FORM 404-1 and 404-2 exist and produce nothing; settle where they live before buildingConsider

Both are numbered, revised (404-1 is at V2, DCR 0022) and real — two of the seven forms D-021 found marked marked as living in Keychain while in fact being worked on paper. Building a supplier module without deciding whether these are its screens, or whether Keychain keeps them and ReadyDoc holds the qualification decision, produces a third place the same fact can live. That is Daniela’s decision, not a build decision.

W2-24V4 puts laboratories in the title and the procedure, and never in the ScopeMust

V4’s only substantive change is a new section G, Laboratory Qualification, and a new title. Section II, SCOPE, is untouched:

“The procedure described here applies to all the supplier register of raw materials and packaging that comes in contact with raw materials, in-process materials or final products.”

A laboratory supplies neither. The document’s stated scope excludes the thing its title now promises, and anyone applying it as written may leave laboratories out. One sentence to fix, and it has to happen before the finding below means anything.

W2-25The entire laboratory qualification programme is two sentencesMust

Verbatim, in full:

“Laboratories will be approved upon receipt of ISO certification.
An audit report will be requested and kept on file to ensure continued improvement.”

That is all of it. It names no standard — “ISO certification” is not a requirement, and ISO 9001 is a management-system certificate, not evidence a laboratory can run the test; the one that matters is ISO/IEC 17025, and it matters per scope of accreditation (a lab accredited for heavy metals is not thereby accredited for Salmonella). No scope check. No expiry and no re-approval, though accreditations lapse. No owner, no form, no record — “an audit report will be requested” by whom, by when, filed where.

This is the document half of W2-06, and the more serious half. the step that grades a lab result grades every lab result on the reasoning that “it is the accredited party” — and this is the document that was supposed to establish that.

W2-26The SOP cites a form that does not existMust

Section VI lists three forms. FORM 404-1 (V2) and 404-2 (V1) are in the Master Index. “Vendor Audit Summary Form 404-3” is in neither the Master Index nor any of the 159 rows of the DCR log.

And section V.C.A.I requires vendor audits, and IV.B makes QA responsible “to schedule and perform audits of vendors”. The SOP requires an activity, names the form it is recorded on, and that form has never been issued. Issue 404-3 or remove the requirement — not neither.

W2-27V4’s own footer still carries the old titleShould

Every V4 page header reads SUPPLIER AND LABORATORY QUALIFICATION. The footer on the same pages reads SOP 404 SUPPLIER QUALIFICATION. The title change is half-applied inside the document — the local version of W2-02.

W2-28Two systems named and never defined, and a definition that contradicts its own useShould

“QVS” — V.E.C: “will enter vendor performance issues into the QVS.” Used once, defined nowhere, expanded nowhere. “Qualified Supplier Database” — named in Record Keeping, never defined, and no such database exists. “QVL” is defined in III.B as “Approved Supplier List” and used throughout as “Qualified Vendor List”.

W2-29An annual vendor review is required and nothing schedules itShould

IV.B makes QA responsible for “performing annual vendor reviews and reports”; V.F.A refers to “annual vendor monitoring”. A stated cadence with no quality schedule, no work order and no record — the same shape as the EMP obligations in W2-45.

Pair it with the supplier module: this is the ongoing-performance-review leg, and Quality Schedules already generates exactly this kind of dated work.

W2-30Spelling and grammarConsider

Real, and few. “Powder-may recommend” (V.F.A — missing “Ops”); “pre-assessed prior purchasing” and “prior ordering” (missing “to”); “The manufactures quality systems” (should be manufacturer’s); “Q.A. Approval: Date:” in the signature block.

W2-31The SOP’s own scope excludes the equipment the auditor asked aboutMust

W2-32Eight kinds of record, not one form numberMust

“Implement a change control system to manage modifications to qualified facilities or equipment.” SOP 434 is the plant’s change-control SOP for equipment, and 421 never mentions it.

Two documents requiring change control over the same assets, neither referencing the other, is the ambiguity NC 4.3.9 is about — and it means widening change control has to reconcile 421 and 434, not just widen 434.

Part 4 · SOP 434 — Chemical, Equipment and Services Approval

“While requirements for equipment changes were defined per SOP 434, change control procedures that include processes, software, utility, and physical plant changes were not established.” NSF/ANSI 455-2 § 4.3.9 · 21 CFR 111.130(e)
W2-12The title names three things and ReadyDoc records oneMust

the Approved Chemicals list is genuinely good: category, manufacturer, product code, SDS number, food-grade flag, NSF rating, approved applications, maximum concentration, contact time, and who approved it, when it was approved and a the review-due date. An approval record with a name and a date on it.

There is no equipment approval record and no services or contractor approval record at all. The auditor’s finding is about scope, and the same scope gap exists in the software — worth saying plainly, because closing the document half alone leaves the finding half-answered the next time somebody asks for the evidence.

W2-13the change-control gate is a real change-control gate, over a very narrow sliceMust

This is the good news and the exact size of it. the change-control gate parks a changed QMS form definition, a changed scale tolerance and — since Wave 1 — the ATP limit, serving the last approved version until Document Control approves the new one, and raising a DCR automatically.

A working change-control mechanism with an approval gate and an audit trail. It is also the whole of it: it does not see a process change, a utility change, a physical-plant change, or a software deployment. Widening it is the buildable half of this nonconformance.

Note the interlock: software change control is named in both § 4.3.9 and § 4.4.39 (electronic records validation). Building it once answers both — and § 4.4.39 is the finding that names ReadyDoc directly.

W2-14111.130(e) requires QC approval on every change; the DCR flow does not enforce oneShould

The current flow routes a parked change to Document Control. The regulation requires quality-control approval — a different signature, by a different person, for a different reason. Today one approval closes the gate.

Part 5 · SOP 604 — Environmental Monitoring Program

“Surface testing results were not available … as testing had not been conducted as established per SOP 604 — Environmental Monitoring Program — V2 — 6/29/26.” NSF/ANSI 455-2 § 4.5.84
W2-16Completing a quality schedule files no recordMust

quality-schedules.js writes exactly two tables: work orders and Quality Schedules. There is no result record — no site, no test, no number, no pass or fail, no lab report tied to a requirement.

So a swab that was never taken and a swab whose result went nowhere are indistinguishable. That is the auditor’s finding stated in software terms, and it is the same defect that put QA’s Light Inspection and Brittle Plastic records months behind: the task completed, and the controlled record it existed to produce was never filed.

W2-17The form’s alert and action limits are transcribed, and nothing is graded against themMust

the transcribed FORM 604-01 carries the real numbers, faithfully: potable water TAB alert >500 CFU/mL / action >1000; total coliforms Present/100 mL; free chlorine >2.0 ppm; Zone 1 surfaces TAB >300 / >1000 CFU/cm², yeast and mould >150 / >500; Salmonella and Listeria Present in sample across Zones 2, 3 and 4.

No code compares anything to any of them, because there is no result to compare.

This is the ATP 35 RLU state exactly as it was three days ago — a critical number living correctly in one place and enforcing nothing. The difference is that the fix now has a working precedent, which is the strongest argument for doing SOP 604 next.

W2-18The form’s water section has no schedule — and the water work is happening anywayMust

Six schedules are seeded from FORM 604-01: compressed air, Zone 1 equipment surfaces, Zone 1 room surfaces, Zones 2, 3 and 4. The water section is not among them — monthly potable water testing, three named tests, each with limits, and a chlorine sample point “close to where the water comes into the building”.

Confirmed by the plant, 27 Aug: the tap water testing IS being done, and the completions are in the Quality Schedules module. So this is not missed work — and that is the point of the finding. The pre-existing monthly “Tap Water Testing” schedule is carrying it, and that schedule was seeded before the form was transcribed: it names none of the three tests, cites none of the limits, and never references FORM 604-01. Its steps say “record pass/fail” against a specification it does not state, and the points it names are the restrooms and the kitchen — not the incoming-water point the form specifies for chlorine.

So the work is real and the record is not. A completed schedule is a work order; it can carry notes and an attached lab report, and very likely does. What it cannot do is say “Total Coliforms, potable water, March, absent, within the Present/100 mL action limit” in a form anything can read. SOP 604 § 5.6 requires this data to be tracked and trended and § 5.7 requires an annual review of the trend — neither is possible against PDFs attached to tasks. That is W2-16 and W2-17 restated as the plant’s own requirement rather than an auditor’s.

The second assumes the first is a requirement. Make 5.7 shall — as written, the plant’s own verification of its monitoring programme is optional, and SQF asks for verification of the programme, not only of the samples.
