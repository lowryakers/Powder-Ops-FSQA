Powder Ops FSQA · V2 · what changes and what does not

One Spine,
Not Fifty Logs

What the system looks like today, what it becomes, and — the part worth saying first — how little of it the floor will notice.

This is not a rebuild. Same app, same screens, same URL, same login. Nobody learns a new system. The platform holds 7,662 FSQA records and years of the plant's own vocabulary, and V2 is a direction to refactor toward — one control at a time — not a thing to start over on.

What changes is where a fact lives. Today the same fact is written in several places and they drift. V2 gives every fact exactly one owner.

Today — a fact with three homes

Take one real question: was the line clean before the run? The plant answers it in three places, through three different doors, and none of the three carries the number the Food Safety Plan says it turns on.

This is not a cleaning problem. It is the shape of the whole system. Counted across FSQA scope: 34 files write a signature, 57 tables hold records, ~50 modules each with its own idea of what "done" means. Every recurring defect in four years reads the same way — a fact that existed in more than one place, and the two copies disagreed.

V2 — one path, and every screen is a lens on it

Nothing above disappears. The Task Center, the Sanitation log and the Production Log all stay exactly where they are. What changes is that they stop being three sources of truth and become three views of one.

The same reading, before and after

This is not a thought experiment. The grading module is built and was exercised over HTTP against a fresh database this week.

How it actually arrives

Not a switchover. One control at a time, each taken the whole way down the spine — and the first one is cleaning, because it is daily, universal, and everybody already does it.

Vocabulary flips at once; records and habits do not. Room names, form numbers, what "complete" means — those cannot be half-migrated, because the schedule and the Production Log once kept separate room lists long enough that a shift could be scheduled in a room it could not be reported in.

Everything else moves one control at a time. You learn the shape once, on the morning clean. The second control is then a variation, not a new system — and that is the training story too: you train the spine, not fifty modules.

Where it stands right now. 26 obligations, 117 findings reconciled, nothing deployed. The ATP grading is built and verified end to end. The four preventive controls are transcribed from Protocol 003. Both plans are red-lined against the SQF Dietary Supplement code and the NSF audit. The next thing that ships is the cleaning record with its limit attached.
