Written the day the twelve NSF corrective action plans were approved (29 Sep; the as-approved text and
the draft-vs-submitted comparison are in `docs/v2/car-responses/approved-2026-09-29.md`). Verified
against `main @ fdb88b0`, `decisions.md` (D-128), `obligations.json` (13 landed · 5 drafted · 23 open),
and the counts below, which were measured on the tree today rather than carried from the 21 August
architecture document.

**The frame.** NSF approved twelve *plans*. Every one carries a completion date on or before today, and
the reviewer's comment on all twelve is that completeness and effectiveness are evaluated at the next
visit. So the roadmap has one organising question — **at the next visit, can each plan be shown to have
been done?** — and two columns under it: what is code, and what is a person walking, writing or deciding.
The single-spine work sits *under* that, because most of the evidence NSF will ask for is exactly the
records the spine produces.

## 1. Three decisions before any more code

These are not mine to make, and each one changes the order of everything below.

### D1 · Where does the MMR live? *(4990683-7)*
D-059 (9 Sep, Lowry): *"the MMR issue is going to be solved in Keychain, not within ReadyDoc."* The
response submitted twelve days later says the opposite: *"Each record is held as a controlled document in
ReadyDoc … The batch production record for each run will be generated from the approved master record.
A copy of the approved MMR will be required to sign off a work order before starting a run."* NSF approved
that. `check:ncstatus` still asserts no MMR table exists here (the D-059 state).

**Recommendation: build the master record in ReadyDoc, as promised; leave the *executed* batch record
where the run is recorded.** Three reasons. The MMR is a controlled document — one formula at one batch
size, a revision, a Quality signature under change control — and that is precisely the shape
Controlled Documents, `gateSignature` and the change register already enforce; it is L1 work on a layer
that is strong, not L4 work on the layer that is fragmented. Keychain has no go-live date in any
document in this repository. And the sentence NSF read says ReadyDoc. The gate ("an approved MMR before
a run") can be enforced on the Production Schedule cell or the EOD entry today with no ERP at all; the
BPR *generated* from the master is the Keychain half and can be said so in the SOP 413 revision. If the
decision is Keychain after all, the CAR needs a dated note in NSF Connect saying where the record moved,
before the visit.

### D2 · Who writes SOP 444, and who approves the validation package? *(4990683-5)*
The plan commits a new SOP and a five-part package with a completion date of 25 September. Nothing in
the package exists beyond the two executed protocols the auditor already saw. The evidence machinery
(`scripts/lib/verification-doc.mjs`, the dated JSON runs, the two V1 documents in `docs/verification/`)
is built; the *validation* — plan, Part 11 assessment, IQ, PQ, requirement-to-test table, summary approved
by Quality — is writing, and half of it is engineering writing only the system owner can do. Decide:
Lowry drafts the plan, the assessment and the protocols; Carol writes SOP 444 (the procedure) and runs
the MRP Easy request; Maria approves. That is the D-056 split and it has not changed; what has changed is
that the date is past.

### D3 · The shelf-life policy is now "clients or best-by" — say it in a procedure. *(4990683-9)*
The approved plan routes shelf life through the clients and puts the rule on an MMR review checklist:
no stability data, no expiration date, "best by" by default. That is a real policy and it needs a home —
the SOP 413 revision (MMR review) or a stability SOP — and it needs Matt and Carol to ask each client for
what they hold. The software half is small once D1 is settled (the date type is a field on the MMR,
derived from whether a study or justification exists for that SKU — `stability_justifications` already
carries the basis per SKU).

   non-editability (new: assert no UPDATE/DELETE path on `audit_log`), e-signature display and password

   still retrievable with its history), access rights (`verify:defaultaccess`, `verify:clientinvite` in

5. **PQ** — a period of normal use: the production data itself (records filed per week by module, from

6. **Requirement-to-test matrix** — generated, not typed: one row per Part 11 clause → protocol →
   dated run → result.

   controlled document in the registry).

  revision should say so.

limit — the batch size, the yield limits — owned by an approved document, read at grading time).

## 5. What "the single-spine version" means as a finish line

So that "we are moving in the right direction" has a destination that can be checked:

1. **One signature service.** Every signature on a GMP record goes through `gateSignature` and a module
   sign function; the Part 11 OQ proves it once and the count of direct writers is zero (or each is
   named with a reason).
2. **Every GMP record type implements the record interface** — file, sign, revoke, retrieve, describe —
   including the MMR. New types are one entry plus one branch, not a module.
3. **Every limit the software enforces is owned by an approved document revision** and parked by
   `controlled.js` when the code moves ahead of the document. Today: scale tolerances, 35 RLU, the
   receiving checklist. To add: temp/humidity, EMP levels, MMR yield limits.
4. **One retrieval surface** — lot, product, machine, person, form number — so the mock recall is a
   lookup.
5. **One generator with two trigger kinds** (calendar and run).
6. **The validation package approved by Quality**, and SOP 444 saying that it is re-approved on change.
7. **The obligations register at 41 with every plant half either closed or named with a date** —
   `check:obligations` green and honest.

Items 1–3 and 6 are the next quarter, and 1 and 6 are the same piece of work done once. Items 4 and 5
wait, as the architecture said they should. Item 7 is the measure of the whole thing and costs nothing
but honesty.
