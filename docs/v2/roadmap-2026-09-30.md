# Roadmap to the single spine — 30 September 2026

Written the day the twelve NSF corrective action plans were approved (29 Sep; the as-approved text and
the draft-vs-submitted comparison are in `docs/v2/car-responses/approved-2026-09-29.md`). Verified
against `main @ 2656975`, `decisions.md` (D-130), `obligations.json` (13 landed · 5 drafted · 23 open),
and the counts below, which were measured on the tree today rather than carried from the 21 August
architecture document.

**The frame.** NSF approved twelve *plans*. Every one carries a completion date on or before today, and
the reviewer's comment on all twelve is that completeness and effectiveness are evaluated at the next
visit. So the roadmap has one organising question — **at the next visit, can each plan be shown to have
been done?** — and two columns under it: what is code, and what is a person walking, writing or deciding.
The single-spine work sits *under* that, because most of the evidence NSF will ask for is exactly the
records the spine produces.

## 1. Three decisions — taken 30 September (D-130)

Recorded in `decisions.md` as D-130; the reasoning is there, the consequences are here.

### D1 · The MMR lives in Keychain. D-059 stands. *(4990683-7)*
The 30 September draft of this document recommended building the master record in ReadyDoc because
the controlled-document layer is strong and NSF was told ReadyDoc. Lowry decided otherwise, and the
reason is the project's own rule: the MMR is a formula at a batch size with a bill of materials, and
Keychain is the system that will hold the formula, raise the manufacturing order and consume the BOM.
A master record here would put the one fact a run depends on in two systems from day one.

What ReadyDoc does instead: **the run names its approved MMR.** The Production Schedule assignment
and the EOD MO line carry `mmr_ref` — the approved record's identifier and revision (a Keychain MMR
id once Keychain is live; the approved FORM 413-1 number until then). Warn first, then refuse, the
D-063 `off / warn / on` shape. That is the sentence NSF approved — *"a copy of the approved MMR will
be required to sign off a work order before starting a run"* — enforced on the run rather than by
holding the document. `check:ncstatus` keeps asserting that no MMR table exists here, as a permanent
statement now.

**Owed outside the code, before the visit:** a dated note on CAR 4990683-7 in NSF Connect — *the
master manufacturing record is held in Keychain, the plant's ERP; ReadyDoc records each run and
requires the approved MMR's identifier before a run is scheduled* — and the SOP 413 revision saying the
same. The note is drafted (`docs/v2/car-responses/nsf-connect-note-4990683-7.md`); Carol posts it once its
14 October date is true; Daniela the SOP.

### D2 · SOP 444 and the validation package — the D-056 split, confirmed *(4990683-5)*
Lowry drafts the validation plan, the Part 11 assessment, the IQ/OQ/PQ protocols, the

### D3 · The shelf-life plan *(4990683-9)*
**The policy, in one sentence:** an expiration date is printed only where stability data covers the
SKU — the client's, an in-house study, or a documented read-across — otherwise the pack says
**Best by**, and the basis is recorded per SKU either way.

| Piece | Where it lives | Who | By |
|---|---|---|---|
| The rule itself | A shelf-life section in the **SOP 413 revision**, beside the MMR review checklist — one procedure, not a new SOP. The checklist Keychain's MMR review uses reads the basis from ReadyDoc. | Daniela (SOP) · Maria (approves) | 31 Oct |
| The basis per SKU | `stability_justifications` (D-061) gains a `basis` kind — `client_data` / `in_house_study` / `read_across` / `none_best_by` — and the product's **date type is derived** from it: *expiration* only when a covering basis with data exists, else *best by*. Nothing is backfilled: every SKU reads "no basis recorded" until somebody records one. | Lowry (C2) | 14 Oct |
| Where the gap is seen | A SKU with no basis is a named gap on **Products → Completeness**; the drawer shows the basis and the date type; `master.csv` carries a `date type` column so the proofer can check the artwork prints EXP or BEST BY (extra columns are free). | Lowry (C2) | 14 Oct |
| Asking the clients | One request per client naming their SKUs: their stability data, or our protocol. Each answer files as a justification row with the document attached. | Carol + Matt | sent 14 Oct · answers logged by 15 Nov |
| Best-by by default | Where no data arrives by 15 Nov the SKU is recorded `none_best_by` with the date of the request — a decision with a name, not a blank. | Maria | 15 Nov |

That is the whole plan: a rule in one procedure, a basis per SKU in one table, the gap on the screen
the catalogue is already worked from, and a dated ask to every client.

## 5. What "the single-spine version" means as a finish line

So that "we are moving in the right direction" has a destination that can be checked:

1. **One signature service.** Every signature on a GMP record goes through `gateSignature` and a module
   sign function; the Part 11 OQ proves it once and the count of direct writers is zero (or each is
   named with a reason).
2. **Every GMP record type implements the record interface** — file, sign, revoke, retrieve, describe —
   (not the MMR — Keychain's, D-130). New types are one entry plus one branch, not a module.
3. **Every limit the software enforces is owned by an approved document revision** and parked by
   `controlled.js` when the code moves ahead of the document. Today: scale tolerances, 35 RLU, the
   receiving checklist. To add: temp/humidity, EMP levels, the shelf-life basis.
4. **One retrieval surface** — lot, product, machine, person, form number — so the mock recall is a
   lookup.
5. **One generator with two trigger kinds** (calendar and run).
6. **The validation package approved by Quality**, and SOP 444 saying that it is re-approved on change.
7. **The obligations register at 41 with every plant half either closed or named with a date** —
   `check:obligations` green and honest.

Items 1–3 and 6 are the next quarter, and 1 and 6 are the same piece of work done once. Items 4 and 5
wait, as the architecture said they should. Item 7 is the measure of the whole thing and costs nothing
but honesty.
